Our Changing Planet

An interactive WebQuest for grades 8–9: find out why Earth is changing and what you can do about it.

1. Introduction

Glaciers are melting, seas are rising and summers are getting hotter. Scientists around the world agree that our planet is changing fast, and that human activity is the main reason. The good news: we also know many ways to slow the change down.

Your role: You are a Young Climate Expert invited to your school’s Green Summit. In this quest you will learn the science, explore the evidence and design a plan for your own city.

2. Task

Mission: Work in a group of 3–4. Research climate change and create a Climate Action Plan for your city. Your plan must include:

  • 2 main causes of climate change
  • 3 effects on nature and people
  • 5 realistic solutions for your city or school
  • 1 personal pledge from every team member

6. Conclusion

Congratulations! Here are the key ideas to remember:

  • Climate change is caused mostly by greenhouse gases from burning fossil fuels, cutting forests and farming.
  • It affects nature and people everywhere: heat, floods, droughts, melting ice and rising seas.
  • Solutions exist: clean energy, saving energy, protecting forests, smarter transport and less waste.
  • Small actions together make a big difference.

Think about it: What is one habit you will change this week?

    7. Teacher’s Page

    This page is for teachers. Enter the code to open it (demo code: teacher).

    Learning objectives: students explain the causes and effects of climate change, evaluate online sources, propose realistic solutions and work collaboratively in English.
    Level and time: grades 8–9, CEFR A2–B1. Three 45-minute lessons (or one 90-minute workshop plus homework).
    Skills practised: reading for information, note-taking, speaking and presenting, digital literacy, critical thinking, teamwork.
    Lesson plan:
    1. Lesson 1: Introduction page, vocabulary cards, form groups and choose roles, start research (Resources)
    2. Lesson 2: Finish research, use the Eco-score tool, build the Climate Action Plan
    3. Lesson 3: Presentations, peer feedback, quiz, self-assessment, certificates
    Differentiation: support weaker students with the vocabulary cards and sentence starters (“One cause is…”, “This affects…”); challenge stronger students to add data from NASA or Our World in Data and to compare two solutions.
    Assessment: use the rubric on the Evaluation page for the group product, the quiz for individual knowledge and the self-assessment for reflection.
    Tips: assign roles (researcher, designer, speaker, checker); remind students to use at least two sources; check all links before the lesson; the page works on phones, tablets and computers.
    Extension ideas: a school energy audit, a class tree-planting day, a debate (“Should our city ban plastic bags?”), a letter to the mayor.
